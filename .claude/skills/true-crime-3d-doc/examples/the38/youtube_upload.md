## Description

On March 13, 1964, Kitty Genovese was attacked outside her apartment in Kew Gardens, Queens. Two weeks later, the New York Times reported that 38 of her neighbors saw it happen and none of them called the police.

That number made her murder famous. It got preached from pulpits, taught in psychology classes, and turned into shorthand for everything wrong with city life. The problem is that when people went back and checked, most of it didn't hold up.

This is the whole story, start to finish. Who Kitty was, what happened on Austin Street that night, how Winston Moseley got caught, how one lunch between a police commissioner and a newspaper editor created the "38 witnesses," and what researchers, reporters and Kitty's own brother found decades later.
